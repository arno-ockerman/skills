# Social Media Strategy

Use this skill to audit a creator, coach, or small-business account; compare it with a benchmark account; and turn the findings into an executable content and conversion system.

## Outcomes

Produce a plan that answers:

1. Who is the account for?
2. What outcome does it promise, and which objections does it remove?
3. What proof and unique mechanism support the promise?
4. Does the profile, grid, link destination, and CTA tell one coherent story?
5. What content should be recorded, published, and measured next?

The deliverable should be specific enough to execute without another strategy session: exact bio copy, pinned posts, highlights, content pillars, hooks, CTA keyword, DM flow, filming blocks, cadence, dates, and success metrics.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the decision window

Identify any real deadline: launch, event, holiday, parental leave, travel, or campaign start. Use live date arithmetic rather than mental calculation. For uncertain events, set the production deadline before the nominal date and reserve a buffer.

### 2. Audit the live account

Inspect, where public and available:

- name field and profile category;
- biography and external link;
- follower/following/post counts as a dated snapshot;
- recent and pinned posts;
- format mix: Reel, carousel, photo;
- posting recency and cadence;
- hooks, visual covers, captions, CTAs, and keywords;
- visible engagement, without treating likes or comments as revenue;
- the linked landing page and whether its audience/message matches the profile.

Use the public Instagram retrieval recipe in `references/instagram-public-audit.md` when ordinary extraction returns only a login shell or partial page.

Before diagnosing a reach problem, inspect available operational evidence: existing CRM leads, overdue follow-ups, booking history, and prior scorecards. Separate **not enough qualified demand** from **existing demand not being followed up**; more content or ads amplify a broken follow-up process rather than fixing it.

### 3. Audit the complete conversion path

Follow every public profile link as an anonymous visitor, including homepage, lead magnet, challenge/offer page, booking page, WhatsApp link, email link, and linked social profiles. Check:

- the profile promise, CTA label, and landing-page first action match exactly;
- one primary CTA dominates, rather than several equal next steps;
- a low-friction capture path exists for visitors not ready to book;
- target audience, geography, offer name, and terminology stay consistent across pages;
- price or pricing route, next start date, onboarding steps, time commitment, and who the offer is for are clear where relevant;
- forms capture source/UTM, channel permission, and create a next action in the CRM;
- public links work when logged out and use stable brand URLs rather than temporary share links;
- footer contact details, template copy, grammar, image URLs, and placeholder text are production-ready;
- testimonials and quantified health/body/income claims have consent, context, evidence, and required disclosures.

Treat visible template residue, dummy contact details, broken assets, inaccessible public profiles, and unsupported result claims as conversion defects, not cosmetic polish. Report the exact observed defect and the concrete replacement.

### 4. Audit visual communication

Build a contact sheet from the current grid thumbnails and inspect it as a stranger would:

- Can the target audience identify itself without opening a caption?
- Are face, topic, and promise legible at grid size?
- Is there a repeatable cover system?
- Does the imagery attract the intended beginner, or unintentionally intimidate them?
- Does personal/family material reinforce the brand story without exploiting private moments?

### 5. Separate ingredients from positioning

List all current themes, then choose one hierarchy:

- **Primary audience** — one recognizable person.
- **Core desired outcome** — one plain-language result.
- **Primary objections removed** — usually time, complexity, restriction, confidence, or inconsistency.
- **Unique mechanism** — the coach's named method or system.
- **Proof** — personal story, client evidence, credentials, community, or publication.
- **Primary action** — one CTA.

A profile with many good ingredients can still convert poorly if each element points at a different audience or offer.

### 5a. Choose brand architecture before inventing names

Do not force one new umbrella name to explain a creator's current consumer offer, personal story, future B2B service, and speculative software product. Decide the architecture first:

- when the person already carries recognition and trust, default to the founder's own name as the audience brand;
- keep the proven program, challenge, or community as a named offer beneath that founder brand;
- treat technical capability, AI, or automation as supporting proof when it improves the primary customer's outcome, not as an equal headline topic;
- name a future software product only after its user, problem, and MVP are concrete;
- open a separate specialist account only when it has a distinct audience and offer, sustained publishing capacity, a useful content backlog, and real demand signals.

For one mixed account, give the secondary expertise a recurring series label and a deliberately small share of the content. This creates coherence without splitting attention too early.

### 6. Benchmark without cloning

For a comparison account, identify:

- repeated positioning sentence;
- identity transformation beyond the surface outcome;
- coaching model;
- hook families;
- offer ladder;
- comment/DM keyword system;
- proof loop;
- content format and repetition.

Translate the mechanism to the user's authentic strengths. Never copy the benchmark's name, identity label, wording, claims, or tone when they do not fit.

### 7. Tighten the profile

Return exact proposed copy for:

- searchable name field;
- biography using audience + outcome + objection + proof + CTA;
- profile photo guidance;
- external link destination;
- 3 pinned posts;
- 4–6 highlights.

Prefer one primary keyword for at least a 30-day validation window. Avoid a bio, posts, and website each advertising a different freebie or keyword.

Build and verify the promised destination before publishing the new bio or CTA. A prepared profile pack is not launch-ready until the lead form or DM route works logged out, records attribution and permission, creates an owner and next action in the CRM, and has a tested confirmation/recovery state. This dependency-first sequence prevents a stronger profile from amplifying a broken funnel.

### 8. Build the content system

Use 3–4 durable pillars, such as:

- recognition and identity;
- practical method;
- proof in real life;
- offer/action.

Every post should do at least one job: recognition, trust, proof, or action. Create a balanced bank with:

- exact hook;
- format;
- key beats or slides;
- footage required;
- CTA;
- publication slot.

For a time-constrained user, prefer a sustainable cadence and batch recording over imitating a benchmark's high posting frequency.

### 9. Production plan

Group posts by location and setup rather than publishing order: gym, desk/morning, kitchen, family/community, client proof. This minimizes equipment and context switching.

For each filming block, specify:

- duration;
- posts recorded;
- b-roll checklist;
- direct-to-camera hooks;
- editing deadline;
- scheduling deadline;
- contingency slots.

Record short vertical b-roll clips and each hook directly to camera. Keep one or two posts flexible when the plan spans an uncertain life event.

### 10. Conversion flow

Use this sequence:

```text
Recognizable content
→ one keyword comment/DM
→ context question
→ diagnosis question
→ permission to explain
→ call or offer
```

Do not pitch a product in the first message. First establish the person's goal, constraint, previous attempts, and stopping point.

### 11. Measurement

Measure the shortest path to business value:

1. profile visits;
2. relevant followers;
3. keyword comments/DMs;
4. qualified conversations;
5. calls;
6. starters or sales.

Treat likes, comments, and follower counts as directional indicators, not proof of conversion. Date all public metric snapshots.

## Sensitive life-event content

When pregnancy, birth, children, illness, or family appears in the strategy:

- use it as meaning and context, not as a prop;
- agree on privacy boundaries with affected family members;
- avoid scheduling hospital/birth content in advance;
- do not assume faces, names, dates, or medical details may be shared;
- keep an evergreen substitute ready;
- avoid a sales CTA on an intimate announcement;
- phrase health content as personal experience, not treatment or disease claims.

## Grounding and artifact handling

- Cite claims from live profiles, posts, linked pages, and benchmark accounts.
- Distinguish observed facts from strategic recommendations.
- State when engagement is only a public snapshot.
- Save the finished plan in the user's declared source-of-truth project folder when requested.
- After copying or writing to an external project location, read back the exact target before claiming success.

## Common pitfalls

- Broad labels such as “coach for everyone” competing with a sharper niche.
- Strong personal proof buried below generic motivational posts.
- Several CTA keywords and freebies running at once.
- A niche profile linking to a generic homepage.
- Forcing a new umbrella brand to cover today's consumer offer and several unvalidated future businesses instead of using the trusted founder plus named offers.
- Publishing a CTA before its destination and follow-up ownership are tested.
- Splitting into a second account before there is a distinct offer, demand signal, content backlog, and sustainable publishing capacity.
- Covers that are meaningful only after reading the caption.
- Community images showing only advanced physiques and intimidating beginners.
- Copying the benchmark's content volume rather than its message discipline.
- Treating comment count as evidence of sales.
- Building a content calendar without an executable filming plan.
- Turning a baby, illness, or family event into forced campaign content.

## Verification checklist

Before delivery confirm:

- live profile was inspected, not reconstructed from memory;
- recent/pinned sample size and date are stated;
- proposed bio has one audience, promise, objection, proof, and CTA;
- one primary keyword is selected;
- pinned posts and highlights are specified;
- content bank covers the required publication window;
- filming workload fits the deadline;
- the full logged-out conversion path is checked, including booking/contact links and visible placeholder or dummy content;
- profile CTA and landing-page first action match exactly;
- available CRM/follow-up evidence was checked before calling the problem a reach problem;
- health, body-result, and income claims are verified or rewritten conservatively;
- family/health boundaries are explicit where relevant;
- the artifact exists in the requested folder and was read back.
